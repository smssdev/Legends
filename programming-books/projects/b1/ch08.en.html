Practice projects

Programs that decide are everywhere: schools grade exams, shops price deliveries, calendars know how long each month is. Build all three, and test each one with many different values, including the strange ones.

Project 8.1 · Easy

Exam grade calculator

Turn a score from 0 to 100 into a letter grade, say whether the student passed, and refuse impossible scores.

You will practise: if/else if chains, the order of conditions, || and &&, and the conditional operator.

  1. Write the grading table first: A from 90, B from 80, C from 70, D from 50, F below 50.
  2. Start the chain with the impossible cases: a score below 0 or above 100 is invalid. Why must this check come first?
  3. Continue the chain from the highest grade down. Each condition only needs the lower limit, because the earlier ones have already caught the higher scores.
  4. Calculate passed with &&, and print a message with the conditional operator.
  5. Test with 100, 90, 89, 50, 49, 0, -5 and 120. These are called boundary values: bugs love to hide there.

Hint: if you put score >= 50 before score >= 90, every passing student gets a D. The first true condition wins.

Project 8.2 · Medium


Delivery cost calculator


An online shop charges for delivery by weight, doubles the price for express delivery, adds a fee outside the big cities, and delivers free on large orders that are not express.


You will practise: several decisions in a row, !==, combining conditions, and rounding up with Math.ceil.


    

  1. Write the rules as a numbered list in plain language before any code: up to 1 kg costs 30; up to 5 kg costs 50; above 5 kg, 50 plus 10 for every extra kilogram started.
    2. 

  2. Calculate the base cost from the weight. For the extra kilograms, Math.ceil(weight - 5) rounds up, so 6.2 kg counts as 2 extra kilograms.
    3. 

  3. Apply express (× 2), then the fee for cities other than Cairo and Alexandria: the city is not Cairo and not Alexandria.
    4. 

  4. Free delivery needs two things at once: an order total of 1000 or more, and not express (!express).
    5. 

  5. Try at least five combinations and check each one by hand.
    6. 



Hint: a classic mistake is city !== "Cairo" || city !== "Alexandria", which is always true. Say the rule out loud: “not Cairo and not Alexandria”.

Project 8.3 · Challenge


How many days in this month?


Given a year and a month, work out how many days the month has, getting February right in leap years.


You will practise: a complex condition with brackets, %, and a switch where several cases share one answer.


    

  1. Learn the leap year rule: a year is a leap year if it divides by 4, except years that divide by 100, except years that divide by 400. So 2024 and 2000 are leap years; 1900 is not.
    2. 

  2. Write it as one boolean: (year % 4 === 0 && year % 100 !== 0) || year % 400 === 0. Test it with 2024, 2023, 1900 and 2000.
    3. 

  3. Write a switch on the month. February uses the leap year result. April, June, September and November have 30 days: let their cases fall through to the same code, with no break between them.
    4. 

  4. Every other month has 31 days: that is the default.
    5. 

  5. Print both results, and try February 1900. Many real programs have got that one wrong!
    6. 



Hint: stacking case 4:, case 6:, case 9: and case 11: on top of each other is the one place where leaving out break is exactly what you want.
