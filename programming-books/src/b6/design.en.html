Design Systems in Flutter

In this chapter you will learn to

  • turn design decisions into tokens: one scale for spacing, one for corners, named colours
  • carry the brand’s own colours in the theme with a ThemeExtension, in light and dark
  • build components that take every value from the theme, so screens never choose one
  • prove that every colour pair is readable, with the WCAG contrast ratio
  • keep raw values out of screens with a test, and change the whole brand by changing the tokens

The first screen of an app is designed carefully. The fiftieth is built on a Friday afternoon, with a padding of 12 because 16 looked too big, a brown that is almost the brand’s brown, and white text on yellow because it looked fine on one monitor. Multiply by a team and two years, and the app looks like many apps. A design system prevents this: decisions are made once, as named values and components, and screens only combine them.

Every contrast ratio in this chapter was computed by a test from the real colours of the theme, and one of them failed on purpose: the first idea for a tag. Section {{n}}.4 shows the numbers.

{{n}}.1 Tokens

A token is a design decision with a name: not “16 pixels”, but “the medium space”. Spacing and corners are plain constants:




Five spaces and three corners sound limiting, and that is the point. When every gap in the app is one of five sizes, screens line up with each other without anyone measuring, and a designer can change “medium” everywhere by changing one number.



{{n}}.2 The brand in the theme



Material gives every app a ColorScheme, built from one seed colour into a full set of roles: primary, surface, error and the colours written on each. A brand usually needs a few more: an accent for prices, a green for “ready”, an amber for warnings. A ThemeExtension adds them to the theme, so that they follow it into dark mode and through every animation:






    

  • Every colour comes with its on colour, the colour of text and icons drawn on it. A component never picks a text colour by itself.

  • lerp says how to move between two brands. When the person switches to dark mode, MaterialApp animates the theme, and with lerp the brand colours animate with it instead of jumping:

Watch out
The first version of this screen built each card inside the list’s itemBuilder, reading the theme with the context the builder received. In dark mode the drink names were invisible: those cards kept the colours of the light theme. A widget test that printed the painted colour of “Latte” showed it: dark brown text on a dark card. Making each card its own widget, MenuCard, which reads the theme in its own build, fixed it, and is the right shape anyway: every widget that depends on the theme should ask for it with its own context.



{{n}}.4 Readable colours



A colour pair is readable when its contrast ratio is high enough. The ratio comes from the relative luminance of the two colours, from 1:1 (the same colour) to 21:1 (black on white); the WCAG guidelines ask for at least 4.5:1 for normal text. Flutter computes the luminance; a test computes every pair of the design system, in both themes:








Every pair passes, the lowest at 6.2:1, the green tag in dark mode. Project {{n}}.1 shows this test catching the first idea for the warning tag.



Quick check
  1. Why does every brand colour come with an on colour?
  2. What would happen to the brand colours when switching to dark mode if lerp returned this?
  3. What contrast ratio does normal text need, and what are the two extremes of the scale?
The answers are at the end of the chapter, after the summary.



Professional habit
Keep the design system in its own folder, or its own package in larger teams, and review changes to it like changes to an API: a new token or component affects every screen. And give designers the catalogue page, running on a real phone: it is the fastest way to agree on what “medium” and “warning” mean.

Summary



    

  • Tokens name design decisions: a scale for spacing, a few corner sizes, named colours.
    • 

  • ColorScheme.fromSeed gives Material’s roles; a ThemeExtension adds the brand’s own colours, with lerp for animation and light and dark values.
    • 

  • Components take every value from tokens and the theme; screens only combine components.
    • 

  • Every colour pair is tested for a WCAG contrast of at least 4.5:1, in both themes.
    • 

  • A guard test keeps raw values out of screens; a new brand is a new set of tokens.
    • 




Quick check answers: 1. So that the colour of text and icons drawn on it is decided, and tested for contrast, once, with the colour itself, instead of guessed by each component. 2. The brand colours would not animate with the rest of the theme: during the switch they would stay light and then jump to dark at the end. 3. At least 4.5:1; the scale goes from 1:1, the same colour twice, to 21:1, black on white.
