Chapter {{n}}

Lists, Images and Themes

In this chapter you will learn to

  • show a list that scrolls, with ListView and ListTile
  • show a thousand items, or a million, by building only those on the screen
  • add pictures to your app, and show them in lists and grids
  • show pictures from the internet, with something sensible while they load and if they fail
  • give your app a light and a dark theme from one colour

Most screens in most apps are lists: messages, products, songs, contacts, orders. Most of the rest are grids of pictures. This chapter teaches both, adds the pictures themselves, and finishes the look of the app with themes, so that your app looks right whether the phone is set to light or to dark. It ends with the café’s menu: real pictures, a list built from data, and favourites the customer can tap.

The café finally has pictures! I drew every one of them for this book. They live inside the app, so they appear instantly, even with no internet at all.

{{n}}.1 Lists that scroll

A Column does not scroll: when its children are taller than the screen, they overflow. A ListView is a column that scrolls. Its natural partner is ListTile, a ready-made row with an optional leading widget at the start, a title, a subtitle under it, and a trailing widget at the end, laid out the way Material’s guidelines say:






ListTile takes care of the sizes, the spacing and the alignment, and becomes tappable as soon as you give it an onTap. Use it whenever a row of a list fits its shape; most do.



{{n}}.2 Long lists: ListView.builder



The café keeps every order. After a year there are thousands. A ListView with a children list would build a widget for each of them before showing the first one. ListView.builder builds them only when they are needed:








You give it the number of items, itemCount, and a function, itemBuilder, that builds item number i. Flutter calls that function only for the items that are on the screen, or about to scroll onto it. A test can count how many it really builds:








Sixteen of a thousand: the ones that fit the test’s screen, plus a few just below it, ready to scroll in. After scrolling 3000 pixels down it has built 69 in total, and those that scrolled away are thrown away again. A list of a million items costs the same as a list of twenty. tester.drag, by the way, scrolls just as a finger would.



Professional habit
Use ListView.builder (or .separated, below) for any list whose length comes from data: you do not know how long it will be next year. Keep ListView(children: ...) for short, fixed lists like a settings page.



{{n}}.3 Pictures and grids



Pictures that ship with the app are called assets. Put the files in a folder of the project, such as assets/images/, and list the folder in pubspec.yaml, under flutter::






Indentation matters in this file: assets: is two spaces in, under flutter:, and each folder four spaces in, after a dash. After changing pubspec.yaml, stop the app and run it again: a hot reload does not pick up new assets. Then Image.asset('assets/images/latte.png') shows the picture.



Here the menu is shown as a grid, with a picture on each card:













    

  • GridView.builder is to grids what ListView.builder is to lists: it builds only the cards on the screen.
    • 

  • The gridDelegate decides the shape of the grid. SliverGridDelegateWithMaxCrossAxisExtent(maxCrossAxisExtent: 200) means “as many columns as fit, none wider than 200 pixels”: two on a phone, four on a tablet. This is the grid you built by hand in Chapter {{n-1}}’s challenge, in one line. childAspectRatio: 0.8 makes each card a little taller than it is wide.
    • 

  • fit: BoxFit.cover fills the picture’s space completely and crops what does not fit; BoxFit.contain would show all of the picture and leave empty bands. For photos in cards, cover is almost always right.
    • 

  • clipBehavior: Clip.antiAlias on the Card cuts the picture to the card’s rounded corners.
    • 




Tip
Phones have screens of different sharpness. Flutter can pick a sharper version of each picture automatically: put a twice-as-large copy in assets/images/2.0x/ with the same name, and phones with sharp screens use it. One set of pictures, at the size you show them, is fine to start with.



{{n}}.4 Pictures from the internet



Pictures of products, of users and of news usually come from a server. Image.network shows them. Because the network is slow and sometimes absent, give it two extra builders: what to show while the picture loads, and what to show if it cannot:






loadingBuilder is called again and again while the bytes arrive; progress is null when the picture is complete, and then the picture itself (child) is shown. errorBuilder is called when loading fails: a grey box with an icon is far better than a red error or an empty hole. A test proves the fallback works, because tests never reach the internet: every request in a widget test fails, which is exactly what we want to check.








pumpAndSettle keeps pumping frames until nothing changes any more: here, until the failed request has been handled and the fallback shown.



Watch out
An Android app needs permission to use the internet. flutter create gives it only to debug builds, so an app that loads pictures perfectly while you develop shows none in the release you publish. Add <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.INTERNET"/> to android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml, above <application. Chapter 11 does this for real.

{{n}}.5 Light and dark



Many people set their phone to dark mode, and expect every app to follow. Flutter makes it almost free: give MaterialApp a theme and a darkTheme, and it uses the one that matches the phone. This app adds a switch to try both:













Both themes grow from the same seed colour, brown; brightness: Brightness.dark asks for the dark version of the scheme, with light text on dark surfaces, chosen to stay readable. themeMode picks which theme is used: ThemeMode.system, the default, follows the phone; light and dark force one. Every widget in the screenshot changed colour by itself, because none of them chose a colour of its own: they all ask the theme. That is the reward for Chapter 5’s advice.



SwitchListTile is a ListTile with a switch at its end, and CircleAvatar(backgroundImage: AssetImage(...)) shows a picture in a circle. Notice AssetImage, not Image.asset: some widgets want only the picture’s source, an ImageProvider, and draw it themselves.



{{n}}.6 Project: the café menu



The menu brings the chapter together: a class for the data, a list of drinks, pictures from the assets, a divider between rows, and favourites that the customer can tap, counted in the bar.








    

  • The data is separate from the screen: a class Drink and a const list. In Chapter 11 the same list will come from a server, and the screen will not change.
    • 

  • ListView.separated is ListView.builder with a separatorBuilder: a Divider between two rows, never after the last.
    • 

  • ClipRRect rounds the corners of the picture; a fixed width and height keep every row aligned whatever the picture.
    • 

  • The favourites are a Set of names in the state: tapping a heart adds or removes a name with setState, and the title counts them.
    •

Summary



    

  • ListView scrolls; ListTile is the standard row, with leading, title, subtitle and trailing.
    • 

  • ListView.builder and .separated build only the visible items: use them for any list that comes from data.
    • 

  • Assets are files shipped with the app: list their folder in pubspec.yaml, restart, and show them with Image.asset (or AssetImage).
    • 

  • GridView.builder with a MaxCrossAxisExtent delegate fits as many columns as the width allows; BoxFit.cover fills and crops.
    • 

  • Image.network needs a loadingBuilder and an errorBuilder, and Android release builds need the internet permission.
    • 

  • Give MaterialApp a theme and a darkTheme; themeMode chooses, and widgets that take their colours from the theme follow by themselves.
    •

Project {{n}}.3 · Challenge


Swipe to delete, with undo


Show a shopping list where swiping an item sideways removes it, and a snack bar at the bottom offers to undo.


You will practise: Dismissible, keys, SnackBar with an action, and a test that drags.


    

  1. Keep the items in a list in the state, and build them with ListView.builder.
    2. 

  2. Wrap each ListTile in a Dismissible. It needs a key that identifies the item, ValueKey(items[i]), so that Flutter knows which item was swiped away even after the list changes. Give it a red background with a bin icon.
    3. 

  3. In onDismissed, remove the item with setState, remembering it and its index.
    4. 

  4. Show ScaffoldMessenger.of(context).showSnackBar(SnackBar(...)) with a SnackBarAction labelled “Undo”, which puts the item back at its index.
    5. 

  5. Test it: drag the first item 500 pixels sideways, pumpAndSettle, check that it is gone, tap “Undo”, and check that it is back.
    6. 



Hint: this is where keys, mentioned in Chapter 5, become essential. Without the key, Flutter matches rows by their position, and after a removal it would think the next item had been swiped.
