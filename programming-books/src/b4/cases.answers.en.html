Do not remove the job when claiming it; move it instead: in the same atomic script, take the due job and re-add it with a new score of “now + 5 minutes” (a lease), then delete it only after the reminder is sent. If the worker crashes, the job becomes due again after the lease and another worker sends it. The new risk is a duplicate: a worker that sent the reminder but crashed before deleting the job causes a second reminder. Make sending idempotent (record the reminder as sent in the database, keyed by the booking, and skip it if already recorded), and the duplicate is avoided except in the narrowest window.
  4. Requirements: owners see a live count of visitors on their booking page; approximate is fine; nothing is stored long term. Numbers: at peak perhaps 5,000 pages open across the platform, each sending a heartbeat every 15 seconds (about 330 writes a second), read by a few hundred owners’ dashboards. Interface: the page sends POST /presence/{business} every 15 s; the dashboard reads GET /presence/{business} or listens on a WebSocket. Data: per business, a Redis sorted set of visitor ids scored by last heartbeat time. Design: heartbeats go to the API, which updates Redis; the count is the number of members with a score in the last 30 s. The hard part: expiring visitors who left without saying so, which the time-scored set does by itself (remove members older than 30 s before counting). Trade-offs: counts are approximate by up to 30 s; if Redis is down, the widget shows nothing rather than a wrong number; visitor ids are random per tab, never personal data.
