1. “Fast”: 95% of booking pages answer in under 300 ms, and 99% in under 1 s, measured at the server over a day. “Not lose data”: a confirmed booking survives the loss of any one server; backups every hour, kept 30 days, and a restore tried every month (at most one hour of data lost, back online within four hours). “Lots of users”: 2,000 businesses and 85 requests a second at peak in year one, with a test showing the system handles four times that. Each one now names a number, how it is measured, and therefore what must be built.
  2. Because back-to-back appointments touch at one instant. With [start, end), 10:00–10:30 and 10:30–11:00 share no instant, so both can be booked. With [start, end], both contain 10:30 exactly, so the exclusion constraint would see them as overlapping and refuse the second: the salon could never book Mona for two appointments in a row. Half-open ranges also add up cleanly: a day split into slots has no gaps and no overlaps.
  3. Requests a day become 36,000,000 (ten times more); the average about 417 a second; the peak with a factor of 10 about 4,170 a second; the table about 34 GB a year. What changes: the database still holds the data easily, but 4,000 requests a second needs several application servers behind a load balancer, caching for the public slot pages (which are read far more than written), connection pooling, and perhaps read replicas. What does not change: the stories, the invariants and the exclusion constraint. That is why the numbers belong in the spec: they decide the architecture, while the rules stay the same.
  4. All three. In the spec, as a criterion: “Given a haircut takes 30 minutes and is followed by a 10-minute gap, when Sara books Mona at 10:00, then the next slot offered for Mona is 10:40.” In the schema, so it cannot be bypassed: add buffer_minutes to services and store the booking’s during as the appointment plus the buffer (or add a second range column blocked used by the exclusion constraint, so emails still show 10:00–10:30). The application computes the offered slots from the same rule, but correctness stays with the constraint.
  5. Both clicks reach the server; both checks for free slots may pass; both inserts reach PostgreSQL. The constraint lets the first commit and makes the second fail with an exclusion violation (error code 23P01). The API turns that error into a 409 with a clear Problem (“This time was just taken”), and the page shows it in place, offers the nearest free slots for the same staff member and service, and keeps what the customer typed. Criterion: “Given Karim and Nour submit Mona at 10:30 at the same moment, when Karim’s booking is saved first, then Nour sees ‘10:30 was just taken’, with the next three free times for Mona, and her name and email still filled in; no email is sent to Nour.”
