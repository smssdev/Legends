1. Each query costs its round trip plus its work: 1 + 0.5 = 1.5 ms, and twelve of them in sequence cost about 18 ms, of which 12 ms is pure waiting on the network. First, cut the number of round trips: combine queries (a join, or WHERE id = ANY($1) instead of one query per item), and run the independent ones in parallel (Promise.all), which brings the time close to that of the slowest single query. Making each query faster would save at most the 6 ms of work.
  2. Each copy is a separate process, and on a machine with four cores the operating system runs up to four of them truly in parallel, so three copies each delivered their 95 requests a second. A fourth copy would share the four cores with the other copies, the balancer and the load generator, which already use some of the CPU, so the copies would take turns on the same cores instead of adding new capacity. In production each copy runs on its own resources, and this limit disappears (the next one appears elsewhere).
  3. By Little’s law, the jobs in progress are L = λ × W = 30 × 2 = 60, so at least 60 workers are busy all the time just to keep up. There should be more, because at 100% utilisation any burst or slower job makes the queue grow without limit (section {{n}}.3): aim for perhaps 75 to 90, or as many as keep utilisation around 70 to 80%, and measure the queue length to adjust.
  4. A logo uploaded through one copy lands on that copy’s disk. Requests for it that the balancer sends to the other copy find nothing and return 404, at random, depending on which copy answers. When a copy is replaced (a deployment, a crash), its logos disappear for good, and backups of “the server” miss them. Shared object storage, as in Chapter {{n-1}}, fixes all three: every copy reads and writes the same files.
  5. Cache the slot list per staff member and day, for a short time such as 10 to 30 seconds, and delete the entry whenever a booking for that staff member and day is made or cancelled; the time limit covers anything the deletion misses. It may be stale by at most that time limit: a slot may appear free for a few seconds after it was taken. That cannot cause a double booking, because the list only offers slots; the booking itself is an insert checked by the exclusion constraint in the database, which does not read the cache. The worst a stale list can do is the “just taken” message of Chapter {{n-3}}, which the page already handles.
