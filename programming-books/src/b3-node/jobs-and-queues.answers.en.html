The confirmation email: a job; the customer does not wait for it, and it needs retries. Charging the card: usually in the request, because the customer needs to know at once whether the payment worked; if the provider is slow or works asynchronously, the order is saved as “awaiting payment” and a job (or the provider’s webhook) completes it. The monthly invoice PDF: a scheduled job; nobody is waiting, and it is heavy.

The job must be idempotent: before sending, check whether the confirmation is already marked as sent (a confirmation_sent_at column set with a conditional update), and pass an idempotency key such as order-1001-confirmation to the mail provider so that it ignores a repeated request. Mark it sent right after sending; the remaining gap (crash between the provider’s answer and the update) is covered by the provider’s key.

Design one: separate queues, exports and emails, each with its own workers (or at least its own worker processes), so emails never wait behind exports; simple to reason about, and each queue scales on its own, at the cost of more processes. Design two: one queue with priorities (BullMQ’s priority option), giving emails a higher priority; fewer moving parts, but an email still waits for every export already running, and a busy export flood still slows everything. Separate queues are usually the better answer for work with very different urgency; priorities suit small differences within the same kind of work.
