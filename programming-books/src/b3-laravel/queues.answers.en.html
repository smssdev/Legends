The confirmation email: a job; the customer does not wait for it, and it needs retries. Charging the card: usually in the request, because the customer needs to know at once whether the payment worked; if the provider is slow or works asynchronously, the order is saved as “awaiting payment” and a job (or the provider’s webhook) completes it. The monthly invoice PDF: a scheduled job; nobody is waiting, and it is heavy.

Design one: separate queues, dispatching emails with ->onQueue('emails') and exports with ->onQueue('exports'), each with its own workers (queue:work --queue=emails), so emails never wait behind exports; simple to reason about, and each queue scales on its own, at the cost of more processes. Design two: one set of workers with an order of queues, queue:work --queue=emails,exports, which always takes from emails first; fewer processes, but an email still waits for the exports already running on every worker. Separate workers are the safer answer when one kind of job is slow and another is urgent; Horizon can balance processes between queues automatically.
